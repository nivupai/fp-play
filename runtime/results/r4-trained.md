# R4 rerun on the trained model: precision levels, FP8 and INT8 included

Generated by `fp-play-runtime experiment --out results`. Every number below comes from that run.

## Setup

- Models: R4's random FP32 master weights (the `r4-precision` model) and the trained weights of `r4-training`, same architecture.
- Prompts (both models): 8 copy prompts `BOS s1 … s24 SEP` (26 tokens). The all-FP32 reference continues each greedily for 24 tokens; it copies 0 of 192 tokens correctly with random weights and 192 of 192 with trained weights. Every policy is compared with the reference teacher-forced on that continuation (192 scored tokens) and also decodes greedily on its own.
- Levels: the five R4 float levels and three 8-bit levels with explicit binary32 scales (`precision/narrow.rs`): `fp8-e4m3`, `fp8-e5m2` (FP32 accumulation) and `int8` (exact integer accumulation), with one scale per weight row, per activation vector and per (token, KV head) slice of K and V.

## What this does and does not show

- It compares how far each precision level moves the logits (KL against FP32) and whether it changes greedy tokens, for one architecture with random and with trained weights.
- The scaling scheme is one simple choice (per-row and per-vector absmax scales in binary32); block scales, per-channel activation scales, calibration and outlier handling are not modeled.
- The trained model's task is synthetic and its predictions are confident; these numbers do not measure the FP8/INT8 sensitivity of a language model.
