# R4 completion: a tiny model trained deterministically on a copy task

Generated by `fp-play-runtime experiment --out results`. Every number below comes from that run; the weights it produced are `r4-trained-weights.bin`.

## Setup

- Model: the R3 decoder's architecture and dimensions (hidden 256, 2 layers, 16 query and 8 KV heads of dimension 16, FFN 704, vocabulary 2048; 2000128 parameters), initialized by the R4 FP32 master-weight generator with seed `0xdec0de0074`.
- Task: copy. `BOS s1 … s24 SEP s1 … s24` with 24 symbols drawn uniformly from IDs 2–2047; the loss is the mean cross-entropy of the 24 tokens after `SEP`.
- Training: 600 steps of Adam (β1 0.9, β2 0.99, ε 1e-8) on batches of 16 fresh sequences, learning rate warmed up linearly to 3e-3 over 50 steps then decayed linearly to 0.1 of the peak, gradients clipped to global norm 1. Every operation is an IEEE 754 binary32 basic operation or the crate's deterministic `exp`, in a fixed order (each sequence's gradient on its own, summed in sequence order), so the run does not depend on the platform or the number of threads; CI retrains on Linux x86-64 and requires the pinned SHA-256, which was first recorded on macOS arm64.
- SHA-256 of the initial weights: `95653702d5e89b2c36e23975554bab10bae94933c6d1433568b9b1bfd18dbc0b`; of the trained weights: `9de08b23ec3ca33cf6650f921de2735188f223cd9582ad5fd8089aa704b15b65`.

## What this does and does not show

- It gives the R3/R4 experiments a model that solves its task with a held-out loss near zero, so their divergence and precision results can be compared between random and trained weights of one architecture; its top-2 margins are measured in `r3-trained`.
- The task is synthetic and trivial for this architecture; this is not a language model and its loss is not a language-modeling result.
- Retraining is checked bit for bit by the stale test (the weights' SHA-256 is pinned in the source and checked again with Python's `hashlib` in CI); the backward pass is checked against central finite differences in binary64 for every parameter of a small model, and the training forward pass against the inference model.
