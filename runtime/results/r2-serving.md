# R2: a deterministic serving simulator

Generated by `fp-play-runtime experiment --out results`. Every number below comes from that run.

## Setup

- Model: 8B-class dimensions (not a specific released model): hidden 4096, 32 layers, 32 query and 8 KV heads of dimension 128, FFN 14336, vocabulary 128000, 8028164096 parameters, BF16 weights and KV.
- Device (illustrative, not a measured or named device): 80.00 GB memory by default (set per scenario), 4.00 GB reserve, 400 TFLOP/s, 3000 GB/s memory, 25 GB/s host link. Link: 2 µs + bytes ÷ 100 GB/s per round.
- Step time: a roofline (max of FLOPs ÷ peak and bytes ÷ memory bandwidth per device) plus two AllReduces of tokens × hidden BF16 activations per layer, each costed from the schedule the collective records, plus copies, swaps and a 20 µs per-step overhead. No overlap.
- Scheduler: 512-token budget per step, at most 64 sequences, 16-token blocks, watermark 1% of the blocks. Workload: 200 requests, Poisson arrivals, own prompt 64–1024 tokens, 16–256 output tokens per sample, 1, 2 or 4 (weights 2 : 1 : 1) samples per request.
- Every run checks the scheduler and token-stream invariants after every step and the KV-cache invariants after every k-th step and the last (k is listed per scenario). Latencies are in milliseconds, shown as p50 / p99 (nearest rank).

With the recorded mix of 1, 2 and 4 samples, swap's E2E p50 stays above recompute's at every tested host-link bandwidth (25–1600 GB/s): there is no crossover in this range. With one sample per request, swap's E2E p50 is 1008.37 ms against recompute's 1028.74 ms at 25 GB/s. Changing only the samples per request reverses the order. That is consistent with a swapped sample getting private copies of the blocks it shared with its siblings, so swapping back in needs more blocks than the request held; this mechanism was not isolated further.

## What this does and does not show

- It shows how a paged KV cache, continuous batching, preemption and the AllReduce schedule interact under one explicit, deterministic model, with every step checked against the invariants and, in the tests, against an independent reference scheduler.
- The compute model is a roofline with illustrative constants, not a measured device. Compute and communication do not overlap. The links have no contention.
- Tokens are synthetic; no model numerics run (that is R3). There is one tensor-parallel group, with no replicas or routing.
- P must divide the 8 KV heads; KV-head replication for larger P is not modeled.
- Failures are fail-stop with constant detection and recovery times; after recovery the group continues at the same P. Continuing at P − 1 is future work.
- No speculative decoding, LoRA or quantized KV. No hardware is measured.
